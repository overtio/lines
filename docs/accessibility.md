# Accessibility

lines aims to work for everyone: with a keyboard alone, with a screen reader (VoiceOver, NVDA, TalkBack), at large text sizes, with reduced motion, and in high contrast. The target is [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) level AA.

## What every part of the app does

- **Keyboard.** Everything that can be clicked can be reached with Tab and used with Enter or Space. F6 and ⇧F6 move between the sidebar, the note list and the note, those showing (not a hidden sidebar, nor either in focus mode), each taking focus where it was left (the chosen list, the open note's row, the note's text). Enter on a note in the list opens it and moves into its text; a click leaves focus in the list. Lists and groups move with the arrow keys (the note list, the icon picker, segmented controls, menus). The note list draws only the rows near its view, so a long one stays fast; each row says its place in the whole list (`aria-posinset` and `aria-setsize`: "3 of 5,006"), and the open note's row is always there to take focus. Esc closes what's open and puts focus back where it was. Focus never drops to the page: after renaming a tag it's on the tag's row, after Esc in the note list's search field (which clears it) on the open note's row, after a menu item that opens a dialog it's back on what opened the menu, and closing the last tab puts it in the note list. In Safari and the Mac app, as everywhere on a Mac, Tab stops on buttons only with Keyboard navigation on (System Settings → Keyboard); without it, ⌥Tab does.
- **Focus.** Focus is always visible: `:focus-visible` draws the accent outline (`app.css`). Nothing hides it.
- **Names.** Every control has a name a screen reader says: its text, or `aria-label` for a button that's only an icon (with the same text as its `title`, the tooltip). Icons beside text are `aria-hidden`.
- **Roles and states.** Built from real elements first (`button`, `input`, `ul`/`li`, `nav`, `main`, headings). Where a pattern needs more, the part is [Bits UI](https://bits-ui.com)'s (menus, dialogs, popovers, selects, switches, sliders, radio groups, tabs), which follows the WAI-ARIA patterns for it: menus are `role="menu"` with `menuitem`s; dialogs are `role="dialog"` with `aria-modal`, a label, focus kept inside while open and given back when closed; segmented controls are radio groups; settings that turn on and off are switches (`role="switch"`, `aria-checked`), each named by its label; selects are listboxes the arrow keys and typing move through; toggles say `aria-pressed`; disclosures `aria-expanded`; the open item `aria-current`.
- **Announcements.** Changes that happen out of sight are announced: a sync problem, "Copied", a note moved to the trash. `speak()` in `src/lib/a11y.ts` says a message through a polite live region.
- **Motion.** With reduced motion turned on in the system, slides and swipes don't animate, nor do a dragged block's drop line and its lines sliding into place, and GIFs from GIPHY are still.
- **Size.** Targets are at least 24 × 24 px (WCAG 2.2's minimum), 44 × 44 on touch screens. Text sizes are relative, so the system's text size and zoom work; nothing is cut off at 200% zoom. Notes' own text goes from 12 to 28 px, in Settings → Typography or with ⌘+ and ⌘− (Ctrl elsewhere), and ⌘0 brings back its usual size; each change is said aloud.
- **Hide toolbars** (Settings → Layout) keeps the bars out of sight only from the pointer: Tab and F6 reach them as always, and they show as soon as focus moves into them; on touch screens they always show. **Icons** off shows a button that's only an icon as its accessible name, the words a screen reader reads, and keeps the icons that tell something (checkmarks, the chevrons of what opens and closes), and shows a note's pinned and locked marks as the words screen readers already read for them.
- **Scroll bars** are on unless turned off (Settings → Layout → Scroll bars). Off, they're gone everywhere, and everything still scrolls by trackpad, wheel, touch and the keyboard (arrows, Page Up and Down, Space, Home and End), so it's a choice of look; on is the default, as they show how long something is and can be dragged.
- **Contrast.** Settings → Theme → Contrast → **Increased** (or the system's Increase Contrast, which **System** follows) darkens each theme's grey text, accent and borders: text at least 9:1 against its background, secondary text 7:1 (AAA), faint text (dates, counts, hints), links and tags 4.5:1 (AA), borders 3:1. The colors are worked out for each theme in light and dark (each theme's `moreContrast`, in `src/plugins/themes/`). The standard look keeps its lighter greys, which are below AA for faint text (see [limitations.md](limitations.md)), except in the accessibility themes below. Nothing is told by color alone (a sync problem has its icon and its words, not only red).
- **Dark panes** (Settings → Theme) take the theme's dark colors as dark mode does, with Increased contrast's, so text in them has the contrast it has in dark; Monochrome and Invert change them with the rest.
- **Changing colors by right-clicking** (Settings → Theme) opens with the menu key or ⇧F10 too, for what has focus, as a dialog named "Colors here, in <theme>" whose fields are named by the color ("Sidebar", "Tags"); Esc closes it and focus goes back. The bar saying it's on is a status, read as its count changes. Colors chosen this way aren't checked for contrast: Increased contrast still works them out to its ratios.
- **Monochrome and Invert** (Settings → Theme) show any theme in greys, or turned light for dark, for people who find color distracting or want less light, without the whole screen changing as the system's display filters do. Both keep the contrast between every two colors exactly (Monochrome keeps each color's luminance; Invert mirrors it so ratios hold), so Increased contrast and the accessibility themes keep their ratios with them. Pictures in notes aren't changed.
- **Paper** (Settings → Plugins → Paper) is off to begin with, and faint when chosen: its color at about a tenth of its strength behind the text (Opacity makes it stronger, up to 60%), so it takes little from the text's contrast. A color chosen for it goes grey with Monochrome and turns with Invert, as the theme's own do. Its patterns and textures are two groups of radio buttons: Tab reaches each, the arrow keys choose, and the pictures are hidden from screen readers, which read each choice's name.
- **Backdrops** sit behind the page, never behind text: the page keeps the theme's background, or a color chosen for it, and can be made no less than 60% solid, the rest blurred, so text keeps its contrast over any picture. The toolbar over a backdrop is frosted so its buttons stay readable. Monochrome turns the backdrop grey too. The backdrops are a group of radio buttons like the paper's; with no picture yet, choosing Image opens the file chooser, and Choose Image… is a button.
- **Pictures from Wallhaven** are described by the words they were found with, as Wallhaven's search gives no description; the picker is the same as Unsplash's and GIPHY's, by keyboard (arrows through the results, Enter to pick).
- **Links to folders and files on the computer** say which they are to screen readers ("Folder", "File") before their name, as their icon shows; following one that isn't there any more, or copying its path on the web, is said aloud.
- **Line marks** aren't told by color alone: each has its label on pointing at it, and a note's own are comments in the text, which a screen reader hears on the line being edited, where they're shown. Mark Lines is a key (⌘⇧M), listed in Settings → Shortcuts. The marks plugins add are only seen for now ([limitations.md](limitations.md#line-marks)).
- **A mascot** (Settings → Plugins → Mascots) is off to begin with, and only decoration: hidden from screen readers, never in the way of the keyboard, never over the text you're writing (it sits in the margin or past the line's end). Whatever it says is said again nowhere it matters. With the system's Reduce motion on it doesn't walk, hop, float or fall: it's at once where it goes, beside the line you're writing or back on the floor, and otherwise sits, naps and cheers where it is.
- **Cursors** (Settings → Cursor): a block or underline text cursor, or a thick bar, is easier to find than the thin one; Blink → Off keeps it still, as anyone bothered by blinking may want. With the system's Reduce motion on, it doesn't glide or trail, sparks don't fly, and nothing follows the pointer, whatever's chosen. The Retro and Dot pointers keep the system's text cursor over fields and notes; for a bigger pointer, the system's own size setting is the one to use, as it reaches every app.
- **Copy what you select** (Settings → Editor) is off to begin with; on, a selection made with the pointer is copied when it's let go, and screen readers hear "Copied". Selections made with the keyboard aren't copied, so moving through a note with ⇧ and the arrows never changes the clipboard on its own.
- **Typing practice** (a ` ```typing ` block) is the keyboard's: its text field is named, its passage is its description, ↓ and ↑ go in and out of it as into any block and Esc back to the note, and screen readers hear how it went when it's done. The time stops while the keyboard is elsewhere, and there's no time limit.
- **Typing sounds** (Settings → Editor) are off to begin with, and say nothing a screen reader doesn't: they're for the feel of typing, never the only sign of something. They play only for keys pressed in lines, under the system's own volume and Volume in Settings.
- **Presets** (Settings → Presets) change shapes, type and lines, never what's there or how it's reached: every control keeps its name, keys and focus ring (Classic's a dotted one, as its time had). Their colors are themes like the others, with Increased contrast's colors worked out as theirs are. Neon's glows and scan lines go with Increased contrast, and Pixel and Adventure turn off their font's ligatures, which it draws poorly. Adventure's notched corners would cut off a focus ring around them, so its rings are drawn inside; Brutalist's are a thick ring in the accent, and Constructivist's, like Adventure's, are drawn inside its wedges. Aero's frosted glass and gel buttons turn solid with Increased contrast, and the shine on its colored buttons stays above their text. A preset never changes Contrast, Light or dark, or the text's size: those stay as you set them.
- **Headings' color** (Settings → Typography) is the text's to begin with. The accent keeps the contrast the theme gives it, Increased contrast's too; a color of your own is used as chosen, so one that reads well on both light and dark pages is best, and Monochrome and Invert change it as they change the theme's.
- **Your own themes** ([themes.md](themes.md)) get Increased contrast too: lines works their darker grey text, faint text, accent (and the text on it) and tags out from their colors as they change, to the same ratios as its own themes. In their editor, each color's swatch is a button that opens the system's color picker, named for the color ("Choose a color for Accent"), beside a text field with the color's value, labelled by its row; the preview is a picture, hidden from screen readers, as the colors are all in the fields. Deleting asks first in the page, and focus goes back to the Theme heading after.
- **Themes for low vision and color blindness.** Settings → Theme has a group of its own, Accessibility:
  - **High Contrast**: black on white or white on black. Every tier of text (secondary and faint too) is at least 7:1 (AAA) on every surface it sits on: the panes, a hovered or selected row, chips, code, callouts, highlights, selected text. The accent (deep blue in light, cyan in dark) is 7:1 too; borders are 6:1 or more; a chosen segment is ringed rather than shaded. Callout, danger, success and code colors are 4.5:1 or more.
  - **Red-Green Friendly**, for protanopia and deuteranopia (after GitHub's theme for them): built from the Okabe–Ito palette. Nothing relies on red against green: the accent and success are blue, danger is vermillion, and the five callout kinds and the code colors differ in lightness as well as hue.
  - **Blue-Yellow Friendly**, for tritanopia: nothing relies on blue against green or yellow against violet. The accent and danger are red, success is blue.
  - In both color-blind themes all text, faint text included, is at least 4.5:1 (AA) on every surface. Simulated with Machado et al.'s matrices, the callout kinds, danger/success/pin and the code colors stay at least 7 apart (CIEDE2000) for the color blindness each is for, where the Lines theme's callouts drop below 1 for deuteranopia.
- **Forced colors.** In Windows' contrast themes (forced colors) the system replaces lines' colors and drops backgrounds and shadows, so `app.css` and `editor.css` mark what those showed with system colors instead: the open note's row, the current sidebar item, chosen options and sections, menu items, switches that are on and ticked checkboxes in `Highlight`, with their text in `HighlightText` (taken out of forced colors, which would otherwise draw a backplate over it); switches and sliders keep an outline, as their backgrounds are dropped; focus rings in `Highlight`; mentions and links in `LinkText`; chips, inline code and code blocks outlined; highlights in `Mark`. Markdown hidden by making it transparent stays hidden (`forced-color-adjust: none`), as do the theme picker's swatches, which show each theme's own colors.
- **Language.** The page's `lang` is the app's language, so screen readers read it right; notes are read as written.

## The editor

The editor is CodeMirror, whose text is real text a screen reader reads, with its own keyboard handling. What lines draws over it:

- The note is a text box named "Note", marked as language-unknown (`lang=""`), so it's read in the device's languages rather than the app's. Drawn Markdown reads as its text.
- Headings are headings (`role="heading"` with their level), so a screen reader's heading navigation (VoiceOver's rotor, NVDA's H) moves through a note's sections. Quotes are quotes. Bullets say "bullet"; numbers read as written.
- Markup that's hidden from sight but still in the text is said in words instead: a callout's `:::!` lines read "Warning callout" … "End of warning callout" (a card's, "Card" … "End of card"), the properties' `---` lines "Properties" … "End of properties", a code block's fences "Code" … "End of code", and a page break is a separator named "Page break". With the cursor on the line, its Markdown is there as usual. Settings → Editor → Show Markdown can keep all of it in sight (Visible) or show it as written (Source), where a screen reader reads every character; Hidden keeps `**`, `#` and list markers out of sight and out of reach of the cursor, so they're never read, while headings, lists and checkboxes are still said as such.
- Checkboxes are `role="checkbox"` with `aria-checked`, named by their task; ⌘↵ on the line ticks them.
- Chips (mentions, links drawn short like GitHub's and arXiv's), copy buttons, tables, images and files have names; images use their alt text (without a width written after a bar, `|400`).
- Audio is a group named by its file, with a Play (or Pause) button and a Position slider read as "0:12 of 3:15"; video has the browser's own controls. ↑ and ↓ stop on them, as on an image; with one selected, Enter plays it and puts focus in its player. In audio, Space pauses and plays, ← and → skip a few seconds, and Home and End (on the slider) go to the start and end; on a video, Tab reaches its controls. Esc puts focus back in the note with it selected. These are in Settings → Shortcuts, with a recording's.
- An image's or video's resize handle is for a pointer only; from the keyboard, the width is the number after the bar in its Markdown (`![Lake|400](…)`, `[Sky.mp4|400](…)`), shown while the cursor is on its line. A diagram's is the same, with its width after the language on its first line (` ```mermaid |400 `).
- Symbols shown for typed characters (`->` as →, `!=` as ≠) are read as the symbol ("right arrow", "not equal to"), as they look. With the cursor touching one, the typed characters are there to read and edit; Settings → Editor turns symbols off for anyone who'd rather hear the characters.
- Menus (`/`, `\`, `@`) are CodeMirror's autocomplete, a listbox the screen reader follows.
- Everything a click opens in a note, ⌥↵ opens from the cursor: a link, an attached file, a mention's note (made if it isn't there yet), a tag's notes. Buttons drawn in a note (copy, format, a table's controls) are reached with Esc, then Tab, as CodeMirror leaves the editor.
- The format toolbar over selected text is a toolbar: Tab into it, ← and → between its buttons, Esc back to the text.

## Spreadsheets

A spreadsheet's grid (`src/plugins/spreadsheet/Grid.svelte`, [spreadsheets.md](spreadsheets.md)), a spreadsheet note's or a ` ```csv ` block's in a Markdown note, is worked entirely from the keyboard, with the keys other spreadsheet apps use.

- One text field over the current cell takes the keyboard, named for the cell ("Cell B4") and holding what it shows, so a screen reader reads where focus is and what's there. Moving with the keys says the cell and its value ("B4: 1,650.50"), and inserting or deleting rows and columns says what happened.
- The grid is a `grid` with its rows, column letters and row numbers marked as such, and the selected cells `aria-selected`. With the letters and numbers hidden (Settings), each cell is still read by its name (B2). Numbers below zero shown in parentheses or red are read with a minus. As it's inside the note's text box, a screen reader may not browse it as a table ([limitations.md](limitations.md#spreadsheets)).
- The bar above a note's grid shows what's written in the current cell, a formula rather than its result. A block has no bar; the text field holds the formula as soon as the cell is changed (Return).
- Writing a formula, the suggested functions are a `listbox` the text field controls (`aria-controls`), and each one ↑ and ↓ reach is said with what it does. (Pointing at it with `aria-activedescendant` instead makes WebKit take focus out of the field, closing the cell.) The argument hint describes the field (`aria-describedby`). The colors of references are also outlines and their cell names, never color alone.
- The cells' menu opens with ⇧F10 (or the menu key), and gives focus back to the grid when it closes, as every menu in lines does.
- What's done by pointer has keys or menu items too: moving cells is cutting and pasting; a block's size is inserting and deleting rows and columns; its name is the menu's Name, a text field above the grid; converting it to a table is the menu's Convert to Table. A table's Convert to Spreadsheet button, and a CSV file's, are reached like a note's other buttons (Esc, then Tab). The corner handles are hidden from screen readers.
- The selected cells' sum and average describe the text field (`aria-describedby`), so a screen reader can read them with the cell; sorting, filling, moving and resizing say what they did.
- Filling by dragging the selection's corner has keys too: ⌘D and ⌘R copy, and the menu's Autofill Down and Autofill Right go on from the cells written at the selection's start, as dragging does. Filling says how many cells were filled. The corner itself is hidden from screen readers, as it's only for the pointer; it's bigger on a touch screen.
- ↑ from the first row goes to the title; Return, Tab or ↓ from the title goes back into the grid.
- A block in a Markdown note isn't a trap: ↓ on the line above it goes into its first row and ↑ on the line below into its last, and ↑ from its first row or ↓ from its last goes back to the text. Its menu's Edit as CSV shows its text in its place, to read or change as text.

## Scores

- A score drawn as marks (Settings → Little Touches) is an image named as it's said, "4 out of 5", its marks hidden from screen readers; with the cursor on its line it's the text as written. Filled and empty marks differ in shape (filled or an outline) as well as in color.

## The Calendar

- The month above the note list is a `grid` (a table) named by the month ("November 2026"), its columns headed by the weekdays (narrow letters on screen, the full names to a screen reader). It's one Tab stop, on the chosen day; the arrow keys move by day and week, Home and End to the week's ends, Page Up and Page Down by month (⇧ by year), as date pickers do, and focus follows into other months.
- Each day is a button read with its full date and how many notes are on it ("Friday, November 28, 2026, 3 notes"); the chosen day's cell is `aria-selected`, and today is `aria-current="date"`. The dots under a day with notes, one for each of their colors, are hidden from screen readers, as the count says it: color is never all there is to go by. Changing month is announced, the month's name being a live region.
- The icon picker's colors (a tag's or type's, Icon and Color) are a radio group, one Tab stop, ← and → moving between them, each named ("Green", "No Color") so a screen reader says which is chosen. Every theme's colors are at least 3:1 against its backgrounds, as icons need.
- ↵ on a day does what a double-click does: a new note dated that day. The list below is the note list as everywhere else, so F6 and the arrow keys reach the day's notes.
- The week or day beside it is a region named by its dates ("November 8 – 14, 2026", announced as it changes), with Day and Week as a radio group and named buttons to move. Each day's heading is a button named with its full date (today `aria-current="date"`) that shows the day alone. What's on a day is two lists, "All day, Monday, November 9, 2026" and the day's timed notes, each a button read with its title and time ("Design review, 10:00 – 11:00 AM"); Tab goes through them in order. The hour labels and the line at the time now are hidden from screen readers. Making an event at an hour or of whole days (dragging down the hours or across All day, or double-clicking), moving a note to another time and changing its length (dragging) are for a pointer; a new one's title is typed where it is, in a field named "Event name" that takes focus (Enter keeps it; Esc, or nothing typed, and the event is gone); by keyboard, ↵ on a day in the month makes a note for the day, and its times are typed in its properties (`start`, `end`), which is all a drag changes. After a drag, what was done is said ("Moved Standup to 1:00 – 2:00 PM"), and ⌘Z undoes it. A repeating event is read with "repeats" after its time. Each event has a menu, as a note in the list does (the context-menu key or ⇧F10 on it): Open, Rename, Repeat (a menu of how often, the one chosen checked, and said once chosen) (a field named "Event name", Enter keeping the name and Esc not, focus going back to the event either way), Icon and Color, and Move to Trash.

## The sidebar's layout

- The Types and Tags headings are buttons with `aria-expanded`, controlling the list under them, so folding a section is heard and done by keyboard (Enter or Space).
- The places at the top are in sections, each a list; a named one's heading is a button like Types' (folding it, `aria-expanded`), and its list is labelled by it. Move to New Section is in each place's menu (as New Section is in the empty space's), so a section is made by keyboard; its name is a text field named "Section name", ↵ to keep it, Esc to leave it. ⌥↑ and ⌥↓ move a place across into the next section up or down. Rename, Name Section and Delete Section are menu items.
- A section heading's menu sorts what's in it: Sort by Name, Sort by Number of Notes and Sort Manually are checkbox items (`menuitemcheckbox`), the one in use read as checked.
- A divider between places is a button named "Divider", so it can be focused, moved with ⌥↑ and ⌥↓ and given its menu (the context-menu key or ⇧F10) like a row; it does nothing when pressed.
- Dragging a sidebar row has keys: with a row focused, ⌥↑ and ⌥↓ move it past the row shown above or below, focus staying on it, and "Moved up" or "Moved down" is said; they're the keys that move lines in a note, and they're listed in Settings → Shortcuts. Each row's menu (⇧F10 or the menu key) has Hide, and Show Hidden lists what's hidden as a menu of named items; hiding says how to bring it back. Dragging is for a pointer; a finger scrolls.
- Dragging a note out of the list as a file is for a pointer too; the share menu's Download does the same by keyboard.

## The Inbox

- The Inbox is a row in the sidebar like the others, read with its count. Mark as Filed and Move to Inbox are in a note's menu (⇧F10 or the menu key in the list) and the menu and toolbar for picked notes, and what they did is said ("Filed", "Moved 3 notes to Inbox"). An empty Inbox says "Nothing to sort."

## Pane edges

- Several notes are picked from the keyboard too: ⇧↑ and ⇧↓ pick the way, ⌘A (Ctrl+A) picks every note, Esc lets them go, ⌫ moves them to the Trash. A picked row says "Selected" after its title, the count is announced as it changes ("3 notes selected"), and so is what's done to them ("Added #work to 3 notes"). The bar above the list is a toolbar of named icon buttons; the Add Tag box is a dialog whose field is a combobox, with its suggestions as a listbox (↑ ↓ to choose, Enter to add).
- The sidebar's and the note list's inner edges (their right edges, or left ones when they're on the right) are window splitters (`role="separator"` with their width as `aria-valuenow`, named "Sidebar width" and "Note list width"), and Tab stops: ← and → move the edge by 10 px (⇧ by 50), Home and End go to the narrowest and widest, Enter back to the usual width. Focused or pointed at, the edge shows an accent line (`Highlight` in forced colors).
- With the sidebar or the note list on the right (Settings → Layout), F6 still goes through the panes from left to right, and the button that shows the sidebar is on its side.
- Settings → Editor → Links is a table: each kind of link a row header and each click a column header (read as words, "Shift Command click"), and each choice a select named by both ("Mentions, Command click"). Whatever a click is set to, the keyboard reaches every link: ⌥↵ opens what the cursor is on.
- The outline (when it's on) is a navigation landmark named "Outline" holding one Tab stop, a "Headings" button (`aria-expanded`). Enter, Space or ↓ opens its list of headings on the one being read (`aria-current="location"`); ↑ and ↓ (Home, End) move through them, Enter goes to one with the cursor there and focus back in the note, and Esc closes the list. Clicking a heading leaves focus and the cursor where they were. The current heading is in `Highlight` in forced colors. Screen readers can also move between headings the usual way, as each heading line is a heading of its level.
- Tabs (when they're on) are a tablist named "Open notes", one Tab stop: ← and → open the tab beside, Home and End the first and last, Delete closes one (each tab says so, `aria-keyshortcuts`), and Enter keeps a preview tab, which screen readers hear as "preview" after its title. Their × buttons are for the pointer and hidden from screen readers; a tab's context menu also closes it, or the others.
- A note open in a pane of its own is a region named "Beside: Finance", with its own toolbar (Swap the two notes, Close this note). Each line between two panes is a window splitter named "Size of the panes", its value the first pane's share in percent: ← → (↑ ↓ for panes one above another) move it, ⇧ by more, Enter shares the room evenly. Open to the Side and Open Below are in each note's menu (⇧F10 on its row), as well as ⌥-click and ⌥⇧-click; a note opens beside the pane last focused or clicked. Dragging a note onto a pane, or a pane by its grip ("Drag to move this note"), is for a pointer: the same is done by keyboard with Open to the Side, Open Below, Swap and Close. So is dragging a note onto a tag, a type, the Inbox or the Trash in the sidebar, which Add Tag, its `type` property, Move to Inbox and Move to Trash do from its menu, each said aloud.

## Find in a note

- ⌘F (Ctrl+F) opens the find bar from anywhere while a note is open, with focus in its field, a search landmark named "Find in note". The count ("3 of 12") is a status that's read as it changes; the match case, regular expression and whole-word options are toggle buttons (`aria-pressed`); the button that shows Replace says whether it's expanded. ↵ and ⇧↵ move through the matches, which CodeMirror announces with their line; Esc closes the bar and puts focus back in the note.

## Photos, GIFs and papers from the web

- `/unsplash` and `/giphy` open a dialog named "Search Unsplash" ("Search GIPHY") with focus in its search field. The pictures are a listbox of options, one Tab stop: ↓ from the field goes into them, the arrow keys, Home and End move between them, ↑ from the top row goes back to the field, and ↵ puts the focused one in the note. Each is named by its description and who made it ("Snowy peak, by Jane Doe"). A status says when it's searching, how many it found, that nothing was found, or that the service couldn't be reached. Esc closes it and puts focus back in the note.
- With reduced motion, GIFs show still: in the picker, and GIPHY's GIFs in notes (their first frame, which GIPHY serves).
- `/paper` opens a dialog named "Search arXiv", the same way: the papers are a listbox, one Tab stop, ↓ from the field into them, ↑ and ↓ between them, ↵ to link to one. Each is named by its title, authors, year and subject ("Attention Is All You Need, Ashish Vaswani et al., 2017, cs.CL"). Before anything's typed, the status says what can be searched for.

## Terminal recordings

- A recording (asciinema) is a group named "Terminal recording". ↑ and ↓ stop on it, as on an image; with it selected, Enter plays it and puts focus in the player. There Space pauses and plays, ← and → skip 5 seconds, a number from 0 to 9 jumps to that tenth of it, F is full screen, and Tab reaches its play and full-screen buttons. Esc puts focus back in the note with the recording selected. These are in Settings → Shortcuts.
- The terminal's text is real text, which a screen reader can read when the player has focus; asciinema's player doesn't announce it as it changes.
- The player's buttons are named in English ("Play", "Pause", "Toggle fullscreen mode"); that's asciinema's, which lines doesn't translate. A recording that can't load says so in words, in its place.
- Its focus ring is the accent color, around the terminal.

## Settings

- A modal dialog over the notes: Tab stays inside, Esc (or a click outside it) closes it, and focus goes back to what opened it. In the desktop apps, if Settings → General → Open Settings is In a window, a window of its own: Esc or ⌘W closes it, and the window's own buttons work as in any app.
- Its sections are a list, "Settings sections", the one showing marked as the current page (`aria-current`). Appearance and Data are headings over theirs, buttons that open and close them (`aria-expanded`); closed over the section showing, a heading is marked as it would be and takes its place. The list is one stop for Tab: ↑ and ↓ move through it, headings too, Home and End go to the first and last, Enter or Space opens a section (or a heading), and Tab goes on into the section. It opens with the section asked for focused. Only the section scrolls, so the list stays in view. Each setting's control is named by its label, and its note is right after it. Privacy's "Open Sync" goes to the Sync section.
- Narrow, the list and a section take turns: choosing a section opens it, and a Back button (named "All settings") returns to the list. Opened on a section (⌘/, the sidebar's sync button), it opens with that section's title focused.
- About → Updates (desktop apps): what Check now finds is said aloud ("lines is up to date."), since the row changing isn't. The ↻ button the sidebar shows beside the version once an update is downloaded is named by what it does ("Restart to update to version 0.4.0").

## Drawings

- A drawing in a note is an image named by its alt text ("Drawing" until it's changed). With it selected (↑ or ↓ stop on it), Enter opens it; its pencil button and double-clicking do the same with a pointer.
- A 3D model (an ` ```openscad ` or ` ```stl ` block, or an attached model) is an image to a screen reader, "3D model" with its size ("20 × 20 × 20"), and says how to turn it. It's reached like the note's buttons (Esc, then Tab) and shows a focus ring: the arrow keys turn it, with ⇧ they move it, + and − zoom, and 0 puts it back, without moving the cursor in the note. Its Download Model is a named button in the block, reached the same way; its reset button is for a pointer, as 0 does the same. A model made from code has its code for a screen reader, shown by ↓ or ↑ into the block.
- A Mermaid diagram's Copy and Edit as Drawing is a named button in the diagram, reached like the note's other buttons (Esc, then Tab), and shown when focused. If the diagram can't be converted, the drawing window says why, and it's announced.
- The drawing window is a modal dialog: Cancel and Save at the canvas's top right, icon buttons (× and ✓) named by `aria-label`, Save's ⌘S in its `aria-keyshortcuts` (in a row above it on narrow windows), with Tab kept inside and focus back in the note after. Esc in the canvas belongs to Excalidraw; on Save or Cancel it asks before discarding changes, and the question is announced.
- Excalidraw's own toolbar and menus are labelled buttons and radio groups that work from the keyboard (its letters and numbers pick tools), but the canvas itself isn't readable by a screen reader: shapes and their text aren't in the accessibility tree. That's Excalidraw's, and lines can't change it; a drawing's alt text is what a screen reader has.

## The PDF preview

- A modal dialog (⌘P): Tab stays inside, Esc closes it and focus goes back. Paper and Typeset with are radio groups.
- Its pages are real text, so a screen reader reads the note in them, page by page ("Page 1 of 2" is each page's name); a paragraph that runs onto the next page is read once, on the page it starts on. Todo boxes say "Done" or "Not done", and math reads from KaTeX's MathML.
- "Laying out pages…", "2 pages, ready", the TeX download's progress and its errors are announced, as is where a PNG or PDF was saved.
- Save PNG opens a small popover with two buttons (One Image, One per Page), reached with Tab after the button; Esc closes it.
- TeX's PDF shows in the web view's own PDF viewer, whose accessibility is the browser's (a PDF from pdfLaTeX isn't tagged).

## Checking

The release checklist (`qa/checklist.md`) has a section for this: Lighthouse's accessibility audit, a pass with the keyboard alone, and one with VoiceOver.

Every theme's highlighter, status and callout colors are its own (from its palette, or lines' own adjusted to it). `tests/unit/plugins.test.ts` checks that they still read: danger 4.5:1 on the note and in menus, success and callout icons 3:1, text 4.5:1 on the danger, success and pin buttons, the colors tags, types and events can have 3:1 on every background, and text on a highlight at 4.5:1 (or as much as the theme's text has on its background).

When a theme's colors change, its Increased-contrast colors (`moreContrast`, in `src/plugins/themes/`) need working out again: each one is the theme's own color mixed toward its text (then toward black, or white in dark) until it reaches its ratio against the note's, the list's and the sidebar's backgrounds. A theme with its own text on the accent (`on-accent`) needs one that reads on the new accent too, 4.5:1.

The accessibility themes are checked more widely, since they promise more: each text color against every surface it can sit on, composited as the browser draws it (rows, chips, code, callout tints, highlights, selections), and their callout, status and code colors against each other under simulated color blindness (Machado et al. 2009, compared with CIEDE2000). A color changed in one of them needs both checks again.
